# Passkeys

Passkeys let you sign in to the ApiPay dashboard without typing your password. Instead, you authenticate with something only you have — your face, your fingerprint, or a device-level passkey. It's faster, simpler, and more secure than a traditional password.

## How Passkeys Work

Passkeys are built on the [WebAuthn](https://webauthn.io/) standard. When you register a passkey, your device generates a cryptographic key pair:

- The **private key** stays on your device (secured by Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello, or your phone's screen lock).
- The **public key** is stored on ApiPay's server.

When you sign in, your device proves it holds the private key — without ever sending the private key itself. There's nothing to phish, nothing to forget, and nothing to type.

---

## Registering a Passkey

You can register **one passkey** per ApiPay account.

1. Log in to [my.apipay.vn](https://my.apipay.vn)
2. Go to **Settings → Account**
3. In the **Passkeys** section, click **Add passkey**
4. Your browser or device will prompt you to authenticate (Face ID, fingerprint, etc.)
5. Confirm — the passkey is saved automatically

The passkey is named after your device and browser (for example, "MacBook Pro Chrome Passkey"). You can rename it later.

> **Note:** Passkeys require a supported browser and device. If the **Add passkey** button is disabled, your current browser doesn't support WebAuthn.

---

## Signing In with a Passkey

On the sign-in page:

1. Click **Continue with passkey**
2. Your device prompts you to authenticate
3. If approved, you're signed in — no password needed

You can still sign in with your password, Google, or GitHub at any time. Passkey is an additional option, not a replacement.

## Managing Your Passkey

### Renaming

1. Go to **Settings → Account → Passkeys**
2. Click the rename icon next to your passkey
3. Enter a new name and click **Save name**

### Deleting

1. Go to **Settings → Account → Passkeys**
2. Click the delete icon next to your passkey
3. Confirm the deletion

> **Warning:** After deleting your passkey, you'll need to sign in with your password or a connected social account. You can register a new passkey afterward.

## Supported Devices & Browsers

Passkeys work on most modern devices and browsers:

| Platform | Browser | Support |
| -------- | ------- | ------- |
| macOS | Chrome, Safari, Edge | Face ID / Touch ID |
| Windows | Chrome, Edge | Windows Hello |
| iOS | Safari, Chrome | Face ID / Touch ID |
| Android | Chrome | Fingerprint / Screen lock |

If your browser doesn't support passkeys, the **Add passkey** button will be disabled and you'll see a notice in Settings.

## Security Considerations

- **Phishing-resistant** — Passkeys are tied to the ApiPay domain, so they can't be used on a fake site.
- **No shared secrets** — The private key never leaves your device. Even if ApiPay's database is breached, your passkey can't be stolen.
- **Device-bound** — Your passkey is stored on your device's secure hardware. Someone would need physical access to your device and your biometric to use it.
- **Account recovery** — If you lose your device, you can still sign in with your password or a connected social account, then register a new passkey.

> **Tip:** Keep at least one alternative sign-in method (password, Google, or GitHub) active so you're never locked out of your account.

## FAQ

### Can I have more than one passkey?

Currently, each ApiPay account can register **one passkey**. If you want to switch devices, delete the existing passkey and register a new one.

### What happens if I lose my device?

You can still sign in with your password, Google, or GitHub. Once signed in, delete the old passkey and register a new one from your new device.

### Are passkeys the same as passwords?

No. Passkeys are cryptographic credentials stored on your device. They can't be guessed, phished, or reused on another site. They're significantly more secure than passwords.

### Do I still need 2FA with passkeys?

2FA (TOTP-based) and passkeys serve different purposes. We recommend keeping 2FA enabled as an extra layer of security, especially if you also use password-based sign-in.
