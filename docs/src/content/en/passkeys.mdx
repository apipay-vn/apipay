# Passkeys

Passkeys let you sign in to ApiPay without typing a password. You simply confirm with the secure method already available on your device, such as Face ID, fingerprint, Windows Hello, or your screen lock.

This makes sign-in faster and more convenient, while reducing the risk of exposed, forgotten, or reused passwords.

## How passkeys work

When you create a passkey, your device saves a secure sign-in method for your ApiPay account. Each time you sign in, ApiPay asks your device to confirm that it is really you.

You do not need to enter a password, and ApiPay does not ask you to share biometric information. Face ID, fingerprint, or your screen lock is handled directly by your device.

Because a passkey is tied to the real ApiPay website, it is much harder for someone to use it on a fake site. This makes passkeys well suited for accounts that protect payment information, transactions, and business data.

---

## Create a passkey

Each ApiPay account can currently create **one passkey**.

1. Sign in to [my.apipay.vn](https://my.apipay.vn)
2. Go to **Settings → Account**
3. In the **Passkeys** section, select **Add passkey**
4. Confirm with Face ID, fingerprint, Windows Hello, or your device screen lock
5. Complete the confirmation, and the passkey will be saved automatically

The passkey is usually named after your device and browser, for example: "MacBook Pro Chrome Passkey". You can rename it later to make it easier to recognize.

> **Note:** Passkeys require a supported browser and device. If the **Add passkey** button is disabled, please try again with another browser or device.

---

## Sign in with a passkey

On the sign-in page:

1. Select **Continue with passkey**
2. Confirm on your device
3. After a successful confirmation, you will be signed in without entering a password

You can still sign in with your password, Google, or GitHub as usual. A passkey is an additional sign-in option, not a full replacement for your existing methods.

---

## Manage your passkey

### Rename

1. Go to **Settings → Account → Passkeys**
2. Select the rename icon next to your passkey
3. Enter a new name and select **Save name**

### Delete

1. Go to **Settings → Account → Passkeys**
2. Select the delete icon next to your passkey
3. Confirm the deletion

> **Warning:** After deleting your passkey, you will need to sign in with your password or a connected Google/GitHub account. You can create a new passkey after signing in again.

## Supported devices and browsers

Passkeys work on most modern devices and browsers:

| Platform | Browser | Confirmation method |
| -------- | ------- | ------------------- |
| macOS | Chrome, Safari, Edge | Touch ID or Apple device confirmation |
| Windows | Chrome, Edge | Windows Hello |
| iOS | Safari, Chrome | Face ID or Touch ID |
| Android | Chrome | Fingerprint or screen lock |

If your browser does not support passkeys, the **Add passkey** button will be disabled and you will see a notice in Settings.

## Security benefits

- **Lower phishing risk**: Passkeys only work with the real ApiPay website, helping reduce the risk of signing in on a fake site.
- **No password to remember**: You do not need to create, store, or type a password each time you sign in.
- **Protected by your personal device**: Someone else would need access to your device and pass its security confirmation before they could use your passkey.
- **Backup sign-in remains available**: If you lose your device, you can sign in with your password or connected Google/GitHub account, then delete the old passkey and create a new one.

> **Tip:** Keep at least one backup sign-in method, such as password, Google, or GitHub, so account access is not interrupted when you change or lose a device.

## FAQ

### Can I create multiple passkeys?

Currently, each ApiPay account can create **one passkey**. If you want to move to another device, delete the current passkey and create a new one.

### What happens if I lose my device?

You can still sign in with your password, Google, or GitHub. After signing in, delete the old passkey and create a new one on the device you are using.

### Are passkeys the same as passwords?

No. A passkey is a modern sign-in method that is confirmed directly on your device. You do not type a password, so it is harder to guess, expose, or enter on a fake website.

### Do I still need 2FA when using a passkey?

Passkeys and 2FA protect your account in different ways. ApiPay recommends keeping 2FA enabled, especially if your account still allows password sign-in.
